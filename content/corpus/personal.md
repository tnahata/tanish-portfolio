## Hybrid athlete

He trains as a hybrid athlete: half-marathon training, tennis, golf, soccer, and lifting. Squats,
deadlifts, bench press, overhead press.

The reason it belongs in a professional corpus is that it is the same discipline applied somewhere
with faster feedback. He treats long runs the way he treats hard engineering problems: show up, stay
systematic, trust the process.

The part that actually transfers is what happens on a bad week. Training does not reward the days
you feel good; it rewards the sessions you complete when you do not, at reduced intensity, without
deciding the whole plan was wrong. Debugging works the same way. The temptation when something is
not yielding is to abandon the approach and start over, and often the correct move is a smaller
version of the same thing.

HybridFit exists because of this. It is the one project on this site built to solve his own problem,
and the multi-plan model comes directly from the experience of running a strength block and a race
build in the same weeks.

## Logic puzzles

Pattern recognition, constraint solving, systematic play.

He is honest that the transfer here is weaker than it sounds. Puzzles have complete information,
fixed rules, and a guaranteed solution. Engineering has none of those, and the specific skill a
puzzle trains (exhausting a constrained space efficiently) is a small part of the job. He does them
because they are enjoyable, and the one habit that genuinely carries over is the reflex to check
whether a problem is over-constrained before spending an hour searching for a solution that cannot
exist.

## Electronic music

He listens to a lot of electronic music, and thinks about it more structurally than most listeners
would. The thing that interests him is that a good set is engineered: it has an arc, it manages
tension over an hour rather than a track, and the decisions that make it work are mostly invisible
to the person enjoying it.

That is roughly his standard for software as well. The site tagline about things being engineered to
make you feel something is not decoration; it is the same idea applied twice.

## Why any of this is in the corpus

Someone asking an agent about hobbies is usually asking a different question: what is this person
like to work with.

The direct answer is that he is consistent rather than intense in bursts, comfortable working alone
for long stretches, and inclined to finish things. Twenty-three days from first commit to version
1.0 on Noiseless, and a training plan that survives bad weeks, are the same trait. The obvious
failure mode of that trait is that he will keep going on something well past the point where a
second opinion would have saved him time, which is a real cost of having built most things without
anyone senior to check the plan against.
