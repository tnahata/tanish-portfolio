## Name and current role

Tanish Nahata is a software engineer. He was at FedEx Corp until the end of June 2026, where he
interned as a Full Stack Engineer Intern from June to August 2023, joined full-time as a Full Stack
Engineer in June 2024, and became a Full Stack Engineer II in December 2025. His team owned an
internal operations management application used by facility managers and district engineers across
North America and Europe.

## What he builds

His work splits across two things that keep converging. The first is full-stack product
engineering: Java and Spring Boot services at work, TypeScript and Next.js outside it. The second
is AI agents, which is where most of his attention has gone recently.

The systems he builds tend to share a shape. Something structured has to be pulled out of something
messy, and then a person has to be able to trust the result. ESMON turns binary recordings off
locomotive monitoring devices into reports an operations engineer can act on. Noiseless runs a
search-rank-digest pipeline that surfaces high-signal conversations and learns from every decision
a person makes on the digest. HybridFit takes workouts that have nothing structurally in common and models them so one interface can log
all of them.

He treats AI as a substrate for building software rather than a feature bolted onto software that
already exists. In practice that means the interesting design work is in orchestration, state, cost,
and human approval, not in the prompt.

## Positioning

He is strongest on the parts of a system that are load-bearing and unglamorous: data modelling,
coordination between services that do not share a clock, failure modes, and the boundary where an
automated process has to hand control back to a person. His instinct on an ambiguous problem is to
find the constraint that actually determines the design, then build outward from it. Offline-first
on ESMON and decisions-as-durable-state on Noiseless are both examples: neither is a feature,
both are the thing the architecture is organised around.

He is a generalist by circumstance rather than by preference. Most of his projects have been built
without a designer, without a senior engineer reviewing decisions, and without an established
pattern to copy, which has made him comfortable owning a system end to end and reasonably good at
interface design he never trained for.

What he is not: he is early in his career, with roughly two years of professional experience. He
has not run infrastructure at scale, has not managed anyone, and has not worked on distributed
systems where consensus or partitioning is the hard part. He writes more backend than frontend by
volume, and he is not a designer, though he has done the design work on every project here because
nobody else was going to.
